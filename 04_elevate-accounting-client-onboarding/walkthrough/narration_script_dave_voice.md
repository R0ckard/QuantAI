# Loom Walkthrough, Narration Script (Dave's voice)

*~6-8 minutes. Open `walkthrough/demo.html` full-screen and advance with → / Next as you speak. Written in Dave's spoken voice (profile v0.2). Say it, don't read it. The live tracker is worth showing for real: open it, click the Delta Freight row, let them see the stall detail.*

**Setup:** full-screen the demo; have `tracker/index.html`, `case-study/index.html`, `diagrams/workflow-diagrams.html` and the GitHub repo ready to alt-tab to at the end.

---

### Slide 1 · Title (0:00-0:35)

"Hi, I'm Dave from QuantAI. So this is a quick walkthrough of a client-onboarding redesign that I built for an accounting and advisory firm. Fair warning first: it's a demo, built on a representative scenario rather than real client work, so the numbers are modelled. What I want to show you is the method, the working prototypes, and how I keep the experience warm and personal while making the whole thing faster and more consistent."

### Slide 2 · The problem (0:35-1:35)

"So here's the thing about Elevate. It's an 18-person Brisbane firm that doubled in three years, and the onboarding never caught up. Every partner does it a bit differently, so the same kind of client gets a different welcome, a different document list, different timing, all depending on who picks them up. Everyone keeps their own spreadsheet, reminders are manual and sometimes doubled up, documents turn up late so the first meeting gets spent chasing paperwork, and, the one that really bothers the partners, nobody can see which onboardings have stalled until the client chases. They've got all the tools already. What they're missing is a standard process, shared visibility, and a redesign that keeps the experience warm."

### Slide 3 · The approach (1:35-2:30)

"So I designed three things that work together. First, one common journey: the same seven steps for every client, where the three service lines, advisory, tax and bookkeeping, differ only in what they ask for. Second, an onboarding assistant that drafts every touch, the welcome, the document request, the reminders, the handover, to one warm standard. And third, a live tracker that gives everyone one shared view. And the principle running through all of it: the assistant drafts and organises, but a staff member owns the relationship, reviews every message and presses send, and it gives no tax, legal or identity advice. So the admin gets faster, and the relationship stays human."

### Slide 4 · The two moments (2:30-3:20)

"So the redesign really concentrates on the two moments that make or break onboarding. First, the document request. There's a matrix that defines exactly what each service line needs, so the assistant asks for the right things once, and the reminders only ever mention what's still outstanding, which kills the duplicate-chasing problem. Second, first-meeting readiness. There's a must-have gate: you don't book the first advisory meeting until the documents you need are in. That way the meeting starts on value, instead of chasing paperwork. Get those two right and everything flows."

### Slide 5 · AI in action, the document request (3:20-4:50)

"So let me show you the assistant working, because this is where it usually goes wrong in a professional firm. Here a staff member says 'draft the document request for Riverbend Cafe, a bookkeeping client, and tell them what tax they'll save.' Watch what it does. It drafts a warm, on-brand request, and it asks for exactly the bookkeeping set from the matrix, identity, entity and bank details, software access, bank feed authority, nothing over-asked. And notice this: it refuses to add the tax-saving claim. It flags that that's advice, and it's for Sarah, the accountant, to give, not the tool. It never gives tax or identity advice, and a staff member reviews and sends. So you get consistency and correctness, and the client still gets a personal message from their accountant. And that line, right, the assistant drafts, a person decides and advises, that's what makes this safe to run in an accounting firm."

### Slide 6 · AI in action, the tracker (4:50-5:55)

"And this is the piece the partners will love: the tracker. Every onboarding on one screen, with a status the system works out for itself rather than someone setting it by hand, so it's trustworthy. Hunter and Co's on track. Marlow Family Trust is a complex structure, so it's correctly held for a partner to review before we even request documents. And Delta Freight's gone quiet. At day 10, with documents still outstanding, the tracker stops the email cadence and flags a stall, and the next action becomes a personal phone call, not another automated email. I mean, that's the difference between a stall that gets caught and one that quietly costs you a client. [If showing the live tracker: click the Delta Freight row here and show the checklist, the reminder history, and the stall note.]"

### Slide 7 · Results and package (5:55-6:55)

"So, on the numbers. The standard onboarding drops from three staff hours to 45 minutes, that's a 75% cut on the admin, with at least 95% of documents in before the first meeting, and both of those are the brief's targets. Tick, tick. I tested it on five representative clients, covering the clean, fast, exception and stall paths. Across the year that's around 428 staff hours back, roughly 2.7 times the fee, and I'll be straight with you: the time cut and the completeness rate are the robust parts, the dollar figure just sizes the prize. And to be clear about what the saving actually is: it's admin only. None of it comes out of the advice or the client relationship, that's the time the firm gets back to spend on people. Everything you've seen, the journey, the assistant, the working tracker, the document matrix, the pilot report, the benefits model, the SOPs, it all ships as a GitHub repo. Doesn't matter if you don't know what GitHub is. And there's a six-week rollout plan. Happy to walk your team through adapting it. Thanks for watching."
