# Discovery and Current-State Findings

*Elevate Accounting. Findings are constructed from the brief to demonstrate the method, not from live interviews.*

## 1. Method (as scoped)

A real engagement would run partner and staff interviews, a mapping workshop per service line, and a review of the last few months of real onboardings (de-identified), then baseline the time, the document-completeness rate and the stall points. This demonstration reconstructs the likely findings from the brief so the future-state design has something concrete to work against.

## 2. Where Elevate is today

Elevate has doubled in size in three years. That growth is the good news and the whole problem: a process that worked when a couple of partners knew every client by name does not scale to eighteen people and three service lines. Onboarding now depends on which partner or accountant picks it up, so the same kind of client can get a different experience depending on who answers the phone.

Six themes come out of the reconstructed discovery.

**Every onboarding is a little different.** There is no single agreed journey. Business advisory, tax compliance and bookkeeping clients are onboarded in overlapping but inconsistent ways, and even within a service line two accountants will do it differently. The firm has the knowledge; it just is not written down in one place.

**Clients feel the inconsistency.** They receive different welcome emails, different document requests, and different timing depending on who set them up. For a firm that prides itself on a warm, personal relationship, an inconsistent first two weeks undercuts exactly the impression it wants to make.

**Everyone keeps their own tracker.** Staff maintain separate checklists and spreadsheets, so there is no shared view of where an onboarding is up to. Knowledge lives in individual inboxes and heads.

**Documents arrive late and incomplete.** Because requests are ad hoc and reminders are manual, the right documents often are not in before the first advisory meeting, so that meeting is spent chasing paperwork instead of adding value. Sometimes reminders are duplicated, which is its own kind of unprofessional.

**Partners are flying blind.** There is no reliable way to see which onboardings have stalled, who owns them, or what the next action is. A client can go quiet for two weeks and nobody notices until they chase.

**The tools are already there.** Elevate is not short of systems: Xero Practice Manager, FYI Docs, Microsoft 365, HubSpot CRM, Adobe Acrobat Sign, SharePoint and Claude Team are all in place. The gap is not technology. It is a standard process, shared visibility, and consistent, assisted content sitting on top of the tools they already own.

## 3. What the partners actually want

From the brief, the partners want five things at once, and the tension between them is the design challenge:

- one clear onboarding journey across the core service lines;
- less manual administration and less repeated client follow-up;
- better document completeness before the first advisory meeting;
- reliable visibility of onboarding status for staff and partners;
- and, running through all of it, a warm, personalised client experience preserved, not automated away.

The last point is the one that matters most. This is a relationship business. A standard process that made clients feel processed would be a failure even if it were faster. The design has to make the firm feel *more* personal, by taking the admin load off staff so they spend their attention on the client, not the checklist.

## 4. Constraints that shape the design

- **Keep the systems.** Xero, FYI, HubSpot, Microsoft 365, Adobe Sign and SharePoint stay as the sources of truth (per the brief and exclusions). The job is to design a standard process and assisted content on top of them, not to build custom integrations or replace a portal.
- **Staff approve every external message during the pilot.** Nothing the assistant drafts goes to a client without a person reviewing and sending it.
- **No regulated advice from AI.** Identity verification (AML/KYC), tax positions and engagement-letter legal terms stay with qualified staff and the firm's existing controls. The assistant helps with drafting, requesting and organising, never with a regulated judgement.
- **Confidential client information** is handled inside approved, enterprise-tier tools with the right controls, consistent with the Privacy Act 1988.

## 5. Implication

Design **one common onboarding journey** with clear service-line variations and exception paths; give it **assisted content** (a Client Onboarding Assistant) so every touch is consistent, warm and correctly scoped; wrap it in **one shared tracker** so staff and partners can see and steer every onboarding; and keep a **person in control** of every client interaction. Prove it on five representative client scenarios. That is what the rest of this repository specifies.
