# Client Journey Map and Service Blueprint

*Elevate Accounting. What the client feels, and what has to happen behind the scenes to deliver it.*

Two views of the same onboarding. The **journey map** is what the client experiences, stage by stage, with the feeling we are aiming for. The **service blueprint** adds the layers underneath: what staff do, what the assistant drafts, and which system holds the record. Together they make the promise ("warm, personal, and organised") concrete.

## The client journey map

Six stages, from "we just said yes" to "we feel looked after."

| Stage | What the client does | What the client should feel | The risk today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Welcome | Receives the first contact after agreeing to engage | "They are pleased to have us, and they are organised." | A generic or slow first email; different from what a friend was sent |
| 2. Tell us about you | Provides basic details and confirms scope | "This is easy, they only asked once, and it is clearly relevant." | Repeated or scattershot requests; asked the same thing twice |
| 3. Send documents | Supplies the documents needed to start | "I know exactly what to send and why, and it was not a burden." | An unclear list; the wrong documents; chasing emails |
| 4. Get set up | The firm sets them up in its systems | "It is happening, and I do not have to nudge them." | Silence; delays they cannot see |
| 5. First advisory meeting | Meets their accountant to actually begin | "They were ready, they knew my situation, we got straight to value." | The meeting spent chasing missing paperwork |
| 6. Settled in | Knows who to contact and what happens next | "I made the right choice. I feel like a client, not a case." | A cold handover; not sure who their person is |

The design target is that every client, on every service line, gets the stage-3 and stage-5 feelings reliably, because those two are where onboarding is won or lost: knowing exactly what to send, and arriving at a first meeting that is ready.

## The service blueprint

The same six stages, now with everything underneath. Four layers: what the client sees, what staff do, what the assistant drafts (a person always reviews and sends), and the system of record.

| Layer | 1. Welcome | 2. Tell us about you | 3. Send documents | 4. Get set up | 5. First meeting | 6. Settled in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Client sees** | Warm welcome, clear next step | One short, relevant request | A precise document list, easy upload | Progress they can trust | A ready, informed accountant | A clear point of contact |
| **Staff do (front stage)** | Confirm owner; personal touch | Confirm scope and service line | Check documents in; judge completeness | Set up in Xero/FYI; engagement letter | Prepare and lead the meeting | Warm handover to ongoing team |
| **Assistant drafts (back stage, staff review and send)** | Personalised welcome email | Service-line intake summary | The document request from the matrix; reminders | Status updates; internal notes | A briefing pack from what was collected | Handover email and summary |
| **System of record** | HubSpot (client), Xero PM (engagement) | HubSpot / Xero PM | FYI Docs / SharePoint; Adobe Sign for signatures | Xero PM / FYI | FYI / SharePoint | Xero PM / HubSpot |

Reading the blueprint top to bottom at any stage shows the same thing: the assistant removes the drafting and admin, staff keep the judgement and the relationship, and the record lands in the system the firm already uses. Nothing here asks the firm to move its data or trust a tool with a client decision.

## The two "moments that matter"

A service blueprint is most useful when it points to the few moments that make or break the experience. Here there are two.

**The document request (stage 3).** Get this right and the whole onboarding flows: the client sends the correct, complete set once, and the first meeting is ready. Get it wrong and everything downstream is chasing. This is why the document-request matrix (`04_document_request_matrix_and_completeness_rules.md`) and the assistant's document-request prompt are the heart of the build.

**The first-meeting readiness (stage 5).** The measure of success is an accountant walking into the first advisory meeting already knowing the client's situation, because the right documents came in and were organised. That is the difference between a meeting that chases paperwork and one that starts adding value on day one.

The rest of the design exists to make those two moments reliable for every client, on every service line, while keeping a person firmly in the relationship.
