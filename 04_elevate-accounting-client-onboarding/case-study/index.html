One warm, standard onboarding, and a pipeline the partners can finally see

An 18-person Brisbane accounting firm had outgrown its onboarding: every partner did it differently, documents arrived late, and nobody could see which clients had stalled. This is one common journey across three service lines, an assistant that drafts every touch, and a live tracker, with a person in the relationship the whole way.

Elevate had doubled in three years, and onboarding had not kept up. The same kind of client could get a different welcome, a different document list and different timing depending on who picked them up. Staff kept their own spreadsheets, reminders were manual and sometimes duplicated, missing documents delayed first meetings, and partners had no way to see a stalled onboarding until a client chased. The tools were all there (Xero, FYI, HubSpot, Microsoft 365, Adobe Sign, SharePoint, Claude Team). The gap was a standard process, shared visibility, and consistent, assisted content on top of them, and a redesign that made the firm feel more personal, not less.

The two prototypes

The Client Onboarding Assistant

A Claude Team assistant that drafts every touch (welcome, document request, reminders, handover) to one warm standard, and requests exactly the right documents for the service line, from the matrix. Staff review and send. It gives no tax, legal or identity advice, and never sends anything itself.

The live onboarding tracker

One shared view of every onboarding: stage, owner, must-have document completeness, next action, and a clear flag when something stalls. Partners can finally see and steer the whole pipeline. Built as a working, clickable prototype loaded with the five pilot scenarios.

The two moments that decide onboarding

The redesign concentrates on the two moments that make or break the experience. First, the document request: a matrix defines exactly what each service line needs, so the assistant asks for the right things once, and reminders mention only what is still outstanding (no more duplicate chasing). Second, first-meeting readiness: a must-have gate means a first advisory meeting is not booked until the required documents are in, so the meeting starts on value instead of chasing paperwork. Ask once, correctly · arrive ready

The line that keeps it warm, and safe

The worry with standardising onboarding is that it feels like a conveyor belt. The design turns that around: the assistant does the admin, so staff spend their time on the client. A person owns the relationship and sends every message, every draft is personalised from real details, and the assistant gives no tax, legal or identity-verification advice. Identity checks, tax positions and engagement terms stay with qualified staff and the firm's existing controls.

Why this wins the room. By taking the drafting and chasing off staff, the redesign frees the exact time that makes onboarding feel personal. The client is talking to their accountant, helped by a good assistant, not to a machine.

Tested on five scenarios

The future-state process was tested against five representative clients spanning the three service lines and the main exception paths: a clean advisory onboarding, a bookkeeping client mid-collect, a complex-structure exception routed to a partner, a stalled tax client caught at day 10, and a complete-and-handed-over client. All five pass, on the right result, a person in control, and the success measures. The five are the same ones loaded in the live tracker prototype.
