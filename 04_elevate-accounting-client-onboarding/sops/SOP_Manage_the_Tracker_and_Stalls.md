# SOP, Managing the Onboarding Tracker and Stalls

**Elevate Accounting**
Document owner: Practice Manager · Maintained by: Practice Manager
Version: 1.0 · Review cycle: quarterly

> **Demonstration SOP.** A real deployment would be confirmed against the firm's processes. This is a practitioner-level operating control, not legal, tax or compliance advice.

## 1. Purpose

To keep one shared, trustworthy view of every onboarding, so staff and partners always know where each client is, what is outstanding, and what needs attention, and so no onboarding quietly stalls unnoticed. The tracker replaces the separate spreadsheets and inboxes.

## 2. Scope

Applies to everyone running or overseeing onboardings. It covers keeping the tracker current and handling stalls. It does not change who owns a client or who makes professional judgements.

## 3. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Client owner | Keeps their onboardings current in the tracker; acts on their next actions |
| Practice Manager | Owns the tracker; reviews the pipeline; keeps the standard current |
| Partner | Acts on exceptions and stalls escalated to them |

## 4. Keeping the tracker current

**One shared view, not personal spreadsheets.** All onboardings live in the tracker. Do not keep a private copy; if it is not in the tracker, the firm cannot see it.

**Update as you go.** When something changes (a document arrives, a stage completes, a reminder is sent), update the row. Prompt B3 turns a quick note into a clean status line; you decide the status, the assistant just tidies it.

**Trust the colour.** Status is computed from the data, not set by hand: green on track, amber needs attention, red stalled. Because it is computed, the colour is reliable, so act on it.

## 5. The daily and weekly rhythm

- **Daily (client owners):** check your rows. Do the next action on anything amber. Clear anything that has become ready.
- **Daily (Practice Manager):** check the "stalled" and "need attention" tiles. Make sure every red has an owner acting on it.
- **Weekly (Practice Manager + partners):** a five-minute pipeline glance: how many active, how many ready for a meeting, anything stuck, average completeness. Filter by owner if a person is overloaded.

## 6. Handling a stall (the red flag)

A row goes red when it is in the Collect stage at day 10 or more with must-have items still outstanding, after the full reminder cadence. When that happens:

1. **Stop the emails.** The cadence is exhausted; another email will not help.
2. **Make a personal call.** Prompt R2 prepares a short call-prep note (what is outstanding, what has already been sent so you do not repeat it, a warm way to open). A human call is the firm's response to a stall.
3. **Log the outcome** in the tracker: a new date agreed, help offered, or an escalation to the partner if the onboarding cannot progress.
4. **Escalate if needed.** If a stall cannot be cleared, or an exception is blocking it, hand it to the responsible partner.

## 7. Handling exceptions (amber)

Exceptions (complex structure, overdue lodgements, migrating file) show as amber with the reason. The rule is to resolve the exception before proceeding: route to the partner, agree the approach, then continue the journey. Do not push an exception forward just to keep the row green.

## 8. Data handling

The tracker coordinates; the systems of record (Xero, FYI, HubSpot, SharePoint) hold the client data. Keep sensitive detail in those systems, not in free-text tracker notes.
