# Workflow Prioritisation Matrix (Ten Workflows)

*Figures are scenario estimates under the stated assumptions.*

The brief asks for ten workflows prioritised across the role families, and three chosen for prototyping. Each workflow is scored on **value** (frequency times time saved times reuse), **feasibility** (effort, data sensitivity, change required) and **risk** (professional, confidentiality and quality exposure, lower is better). **Priority = Value times Feasibility divided by Risk**, rounded. The weights are visible so the Executive Team can re-weight them for the firm's risk appetite.

Scores are 1 to 5.

## Why these three pilots

The three prototypes are chosen to span the archetypes and to prove the model where it matters:

**Project status reporting (highest priority, lowest risk)** builds trust fastest. It is high-frequency, low-risk, and produces a visible, consistent improvement clients notice, the ideal confidence-builder for a firm that is cautious about AI.

**Proposals and bids (highest value)** is where the firm competes for revenue. Faster, more consistent bids from a structured brief remove duplicated effort and lift win-readiness, and the workflow reuses the firm's best material instead of trapping it.

**Technical report drafting and QA (highest stakes)** is the one that proves AI can touch engineering-adjacent work safely. It is deliberately the hardest pilot, because it is where the assurance line matters most: the AI helps with structure, clarity and consistency, and a qualified engineer owns every technical statement. Getting this right is what earns the technical staff's trust in the whole programme.

Together they show the Executive Team that the model works for a quick win, a revenue driver and a high-stakes technical workflow, so the patterns transfer to the other seven.

## The explicit exclusion

**Engineering design, calculation, verification and certification are excluded by design, not just by scope.** This is where AI does the most harm in an engineering context, and where professional and safety accountability is absolute. The AI in this operating model assists drafting, formatting, structure and admin. It never performs or checks a calculation, never verifies a design, and never certifies anything. A qualified, registered engineer owns every technical output. This line is a feature of the model, not a limitation, it is what makes the whole thing safe to adopt.

## How to read this

Priority is a decision aid, not a verdict. The scores are deliberately simple and visible so the governance forum can re-weight them. The matrix is included as a live sheet in the benefits tracker so it can be re-run as the firm's risk appetite and evidence change.
