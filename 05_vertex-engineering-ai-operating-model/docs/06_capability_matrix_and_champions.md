# Capability Matrix and AI Champions Programme

*Vertex Engineering. How good AI use becomes a firm habit, not a personal one.*

Tools and rules do not change a firm. People do. Vertex's adoption today is bimodal: a few enthusiasts using AI privately, and cautious staff avoiding it, with nobody in between and no shared idea of what "good" looks like. This layer fixes that with three simple things: a capability matrix that says what good AI use means for each role family, an AI Champions network that spreads it, and a light learning pathway that lifts everyone to a baseline.

## What "good AI use" means, by role family

The matrix defines a baseline (everyone should reach this) and a target (what a confident, fluent user looks like) for each of the five role families. It is written per family, not per person, so guidance is manageable and fair.

| Role family | Baseline (everyone) | Target (fluent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Engineering delivery** | Uses AI to draft and tidy report prose, summarise source material, and check consistency against a template. Knows the bright line cold: never design, calculate, verify or certify with AI. Always the named owner of the technical content. | Runs the report drafting-and-QA workflow end to end, maintains the discipline's prompt patterns, coaches peers on where the assurance line sits. |
| **Business development** | Produces a first proposal draft from a structured brief using the firm's approved workflow and reuse library, instead of starting from a blank page. | Owns the proposals prompt set, keeps the reusable content current, lifts win-readiness by reusing the firm's best material. |
| **Project management** | Generates reliable status updates from project data using the standard workflow; trusts the output enough to rely on it, checks it before it goes out. | Tunes the status workflow for their portfolio, spots where reporting effort can be removed, mentors PMs on adoption. |
| **Finance** | Uses AI to draft management-reporting narratives and reformat data-heavy outputs into plain language, with numbers always human-verified. | Builds repeatable reporting narratives, feeds finance patterns into the prompt library. |
| **People and operations** | Drafts internal comms, policy and process documentation with AI, applying the firm tone and the confidentiality rules. | Owns the internal-documentation workflow and the fairness controls on any recruitment use. |

Two rules run across every family. First, **the human is always the owner**: AI drafts, a named person decides and signs. Second, **confidentiality is baseline, not advanced**: enterprise-tier only, least exposure, when in doubt ask. Nobody is "fluent" if they are careless with client data.

## The AI Champions network

A firm-wide model with no platform team needs internal carriers. The Champions are it.

- **Who:** one Champion per role family, ideally an existing respected practitioner rather than the most technical person. Five to seven people across the 52-person firm. In engineering delivery the Champion is deliberately someone senior enough to be trusted on the assurance line.
- **What they do:** run short workflow demos for their family, keep the family's prompts and reuse content current, answer the day-to-day "which tool, is this okay" questions so governance does not have to, and feed real examples and problems back to the forum.
- **What they are not:** they are not IT support, not approvers, and not a substitute for the professional checking process. They are peers who go first and bring others with them.
- **Time and recognition:** a genuine time allocation (a defined portion of a day per fortnight) and visible recognition. A Champions role with no time and no recognition is a title that quietly dies. The 90-day plan resources it explicitly.

## The learning pathway

Three tiers, kept deliberately light. The point is confident everyday use, not certification.

1. **Firm baseline (everyone, about ninety minutes).** The operating principle, the bright line, the tool-selection decision path, the confidentiality rules, and one hands-on run through a relevant workflow. Nobody uses AI on firm work until they have done this.
2. **Role-family workshop (by family, half a day).** The family's approved workflows, its prompt set, and its specific assurance points, run hands-on on real (suitably handled) examples. Led by the Champion.
3. **Champions deep-dive (Champions only, ongoing).** Enough fluency to coach, maintain the prompt library, and judge new use cases against the model. Champions meet monthly with the forum.

## Measurement: a baseline the firm can steer by

Vertex cannot connect spend to benefit today because there is no baseline. The programme starts one, kept simple on purpose.

- **Adoption:** share of staff per family who have completed baseline training and use an approved workflow at least weekly.
- **Benefit:** cycle-time on the three pilot workflows, before and after, tracked in the benefits tracker. The target is the brief's 15% cycle-time improvement without unacceptable quality loss; the scenario model in `before_after_comparison.md` clears it.
- **Licence value:** active users per platform against licences held, so managers can finally justify or reallocate spend.
- **Assurance health:** incidents logged and closed, and time-to-close. Low is good; zero usually means under-reporting, not perfection.

These four numbers are the executive dashboard (`executive_dashboard_spec.md`). They are enough to steer and few enough to actually maintain.

## The first ninety days (summary)

The full sequence is in `roadmap.md`; in brief: appoint the sponsor and Champions and stand up the forum in the first fortnight; run baseline training and the three role-family workshops for the pilot families across weeks three to eight; run the three pilots live with before/after measurement through the quarter; and review at day ninety to decide the fast-follow workflows. The success test at ninety days is simple: the three pilots have cleared the 15% bar, Champions are active, and the Executive Team has the dashboard it was missing.

## Why capability is the layer that makes it stick

Governance keeps AI safe and tool-selection makes it efficient, but neither creates a habit. Capability does. When every engineer, PM and proposals lead knows what good looks like for their own work, has a peer who went first, and has had ninety minutes to try it safely, adoption stops depending on a few enthusiasts and becomes simply how the firm works. That is the difference between buying AI and operating it.
