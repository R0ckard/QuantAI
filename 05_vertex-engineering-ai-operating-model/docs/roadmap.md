# Implementation Roadmap: First 90 Days and 12 Months

*Vertex Engineering. From an approved model to a firm habit.*

The brief asks for an implementation path with the first 90 days resourced and AI Champions appointed. This is that path. It is sequenced so the firm gets an early, safe, visible win (status reporting) before it takes on the highest-stakes pilot (technical reports), and so governance and capability are in place before adoption scales.

## Phase 0: Approve and stand up (weeks 1-2)

- **Executive Team approves** the target operating model, the bright line, and the governance structure. Nothing rolls out before this.
- **Appoint the sponsor and the AI Champions**, one per role family (five to seven people), with real time allocation and visible recognition. The engineering-delivery Champion is deliberately senior enough to be trusted on the assurance line.
- **Stand up the governance forum** (four to five people) and adopt the tool-selection matrix, approved-use rules and incident path as-is.
- **Set the baseline:** pull current licence-usage numbers and record the pre-pilot cycle-times for the three workflows, so "before" is measured, not assumed.

Resourcing: Champions time defined and protected; forum diarised monthly; sponsor visible.

## Phase 1: Quick win and capability (weeks 3-8)

- **Run baseline training** (the 90-minute firm session) for everyone in the pilot families. Nobody uses AI on firm work until they have done it.
- **Launch Pilot 3, project status reporting first.** Lowest risk, highest frequency, immediately visible to clients. Configure the Copilot template and saved prompt; PMs start using it; the adoption log captures real before/after times.
- **Run the role-family workshops** (half a day each) for BD and engineering delivery, led by their Champions, on real, appropriately handled examples.
- **Launch Pilot 1, proposals and bids.** Stand up the Claude Project, the reuse library and the bid-brief intake; BD starts drafting from briefs.

By week 8: two pilots live, first real measurements in, Champions active, most of the pilot families trained.

## Phase 2: The high-stakes pilot (weeks 6-12, overlapping)

- **Launch Pilot 2, technical report drafting and QA**, deliberately after the firm has seen the model work safely on the lower-stakes pilots. Stand up the Claude Project with the assurance line pinned, run the engineering-delivery workshop, and start with a small group of respected engineers.
- **Watch the assurance line closely.** Any drift toward the AI implying a technical judgement is a priority fix, logged with the forum. This pilot earns the technical staff's trust or it does not; it is run carefully on purpose.
- **Populate the executive dashboard** with the first month of real adoption, cycle-time, licence-value and assurance-health numbers.

## Phase 3: The 90-day review (end of week 12)

The success test, straight from the brief:

- The three pilots have shown **at least 15% cycle-time improvement** on measured actuals, without unacceptable quality loss.
- **AI Champions are active** and the governance forum is running.
- The **Executive Team has the dashboard** it was missing, on real numbers.

At the review the forum decides the **fast-follow workflows** (capability statements and CVs, meeting notes and actions, client and internal comms, internal policy and process docs all score high and low-risk) and re-weights the prioritisation matrix on the evidence gathered.

## Months 4-12: Scale and embed

- **Roll out the fast-follow workflows** one at a time, each with a Champion, a prompt set and a spot on the dashboard. Same pattern as the pilots.
- **Firm-wide baseline training complete;** adoption target is a clear majority of each family using an approved workflow weekly.
- **Move governance to quarterly** once the model is steady.
- **Begin Phase 2 workflows** (knowledge retrieval and reuse once SharePoint is tidied; finance narratives; recruitment with fairness controls first).
- **12-month maturity target:** from about 1.6 ("Emerging") to about 4 across the leading dimensions, as set out in `01_discovery_and_maturity_assessment.md`.

## What stays constant the whole way

The operating principle and the bright line do not phase in or out. From day one: AI assists, a qualified professional decides and signs, and AI never performs, checks or certifies engineering work. Everything above is about spreading that safely, never about relaxing it.

*Timings and targets are a scenario plan under stated assumptions; a real rollout would tune them to the firm's calendar and capacity.*
