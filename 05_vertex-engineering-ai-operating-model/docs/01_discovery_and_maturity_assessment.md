# Discovery Summary and AI Maturity Assessment

*Vertex Engineering. Findings are constructed from the brief to demonstrate the method, not from live interviews.*

## 1. Method (as scoped)

A real engagement would run executive interviews, department workshops and a firm-wide maturity survey, then map the operating model, role families and high-value work patterns. This demonstration reconstructs the likely findings from the brief so the target model has something concrete to design against.

## 2. Where Vertex is today

Vertex has done the hard part that most firms avoid: it has invested in capable tools. The problem is the opposite of scarcity. It has **three overlapping AI platforms** (Microsoft Copilot, Claude Team, ChatGPT Enterprise) and no shared view of which to use for what, no approved workflows, and no way to connect licence spend to benefit. The result is low, fragmented adoption: enthusiasts using tools privately, cautious staff avoiding them, and leadership unable to explain where value is being created.

Six themes came out of the reconstructed discovery:

**Three tools, no decision rules.** Staff pick a platform by habit or preference, not by fit. Nobody can say when Copilot is the right choice over Claude or ChatGPT, so effort is duplicated and quality is uneven.

**No approved workflows or examples.** There is no library of "this is how we do a proposal with AI" or "this is what a good AI-assisted report looks like," so every person reinvents the approach.

**Spend disconnected from benefit.** Managers hold licences they cannot justify because there is no baseline and no measure of adoption or time saved.

**Knowledge trapped.** The same content is rebuilt across proposals, reports and project folders. When an experienced engineer or project manager leaves, their judgement and their best material leave too.

**Professional-liability caution (the important one).** Technical staff are rightly concerned about accuracy, confidentiality and professional liability. In an engineering consultancy this is not resistance to change, it is professional responsibility. Any operating model that does not put a qualified human in charge of every technical output will be rejected by the people who matter most, and rightly so.

**No governance, no plan, no baseline.** There is no governance forum, no adoption plan and no benefits baseline, so the firm cannot steer.

## 3. AI maturity assessment

Scored 1 to 5 across seven dimensions (1 = ad hoc, 5 = optimised). Scores are an illustrative baseline for this scenario.

| Dimension | Score (of 5) | Evidence | Target (12 months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy and operating model | 2 | AI seen as important but no operating model or decision rules | 4 |
| Tool selection | 1 | Three platforms, no guidance on which to use when | 4 |
| Workflows and standards | 1 | No approved workflows, prompts or examples | 3 |
| Governance and assurance | 2 | Strong professional instincts but no framework | 4 |
| Capability and adoption | 2 | Bimodal, enthusiasts vs avoiders, no learning pathway | 4 |
| Knowledge and reuse | 2 | Good material exists but is duplicated and un-versioned | 3 |
| Measurement | 1 | No baseline for adoption, benefit or licence value | 3 |

**Weighted maturity: about 1.6 of 5, "Emerging."** Vertex has tools and appetite but no operating model around them. The highest-value moves are not more technology, they are decision rules, governance and a small set of proven workflows.

## 4. Constraints that shape the design

- **Professional accountability is non-negotiable.** Engineering design, calculation, verification and certification stay with qualified, registered engineers. AI assists drafting and admin; it never produces or checks technical content that carries professional or safety consequences.
- **Three platforms, keep them.** Vendor contracts and licences remain in place (per the brief), so the job is to use the existing three well, not to add or replace tools. That makes a clear tool-selection framework the central deliverable.
- **Confidentiality.** Client and project information, and any commercially sensitive material, must be handled inside approved, enterprise-tier tools with the right data controls.
- **Firm-wide, no platform team.** Whatever is designed must be run by an internal AI Champions network and a light governance forum, not a dedicated engineering team.

## 5. Implication

Design a firm-wide **operating model** that (a) gives everyone clear decision rules for which AI to use and where AI must not be used, (b) proves the value on a small set of high-frequency workflows, (c) wraps both in governance that respects professional accountability, and (d) builds internal capability so it sticks. That is what the rest of this repository specifies, proven on three prototypes.
