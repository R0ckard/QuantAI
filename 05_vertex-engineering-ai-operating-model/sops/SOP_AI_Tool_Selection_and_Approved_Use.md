# SOP, AI Tool Selection and Approved Use

**Vertex Engineering**
Document owner: AI Governance Forum · Maintained by: AI Champions network
Version: 1.0 · Review cycle: quarterly, or sooner on a material platform change

> **Demonstration SOP.** A real deployment would be confirmed against the firm's data-handling and vendor arrangements. This is a practitioner-level operating control, not legal advice.

## 1. Purpose

To give everyone at Vertex a clear, fast rule for which of the firm's three AI platforms (Microsoft Copilot, Claude Team, ChatGPT Enterprise) to use for which task, and where each is approved, so effort is not duplicated, quality is consistent, and licence spend connects to benefit. The contracts stay; this SOP makes them a deliberate toolkit.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all staff using AI for firm or client work. It governs tool choice and data handling. It does not relax the bright line: AI never performs, checks or certifies engineering work (see the technical-report SOP and the governance framework).

## 3. The rule of thumb

> Copilot for work inside your Microsoft files and inbox. Claude for careful drafting, structure and review of long documents. ChatGPT for open-ended thinking, research framing and quick generation.

That resolves most choices. Use the decision path for the rest.

## 4. The decision path

Ask these in order; stop at the first answer.

1. **Confidential, client-identifying or commercially sensitive?** Approved enterprise tier only (Copilot in-tenant, ChatGPT Enterprise, or Claude Team), never a personal or free account. Then continue to choose which.
2. **Does the task work on your Microsoft content** (email, Teams, Word, Excel, a meeting you attended)? Use **Copilot**; it has the context and stays in-tenant.
3. **A long or high-stakes document** needing careful structure and review? Use **Claude**.
4. **Open-ended** (brainstorming, research framing, quick generation)? Use **ChatGPT**. Still unsure: default to the rule of thumb or ask an AI Champion.

## 5. Approved-use quick reference

| Task | Start with |
|---|---|
| Summarise a Teams meeting you attended | Copilot |
| Draft a reply inside Outlook | Copilot |
| Turn an Excel export into a status narrative | Copilot |
| Draft a long technical report section (around fixed, human-owned content) | Claude |
| QA a draft for structure, clarity, consistency | Claude |
| Draft a proposal from a structured bid brief | Claude |
| Brainstorm approaches; research framing | ChatGPT |
| Rewrite to firm tone or shorter length | Claude |
| Anything touching engineering design, calculation, verification or certification | None. Out of bounds for every tool. |

## 6. The confidentiality rules that override tool choice

1. **Enterprise tier only for anything real.** Client names, project detail, commercial terms, personal information and any internal document go only into approved enterprise instances. Never personal or free accounts.
2. **Least exposure.** If a task can be done without pasting client-identifying detail, do it that way.
3. **When in doubt, up a level.** Treat unsure material as sensitive and check with an AI Champion or the forum.

## 7. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| All staff | Follow the decision path and the confidentiality rules; own every output |
| AI Champions | Answer day-to-day "which tool, is this okay"; feed real examples back |
| AI Governance Forum | Owns this SOP and the approved-use matrix; reviews quarterly |

## 8. Exceptions and escalation

- Unsure whether something is confidential → treat as sensitive; ask a Champion.
- A platform ships a material change → the forum reviews the matrix out of cycle.
- A task does not fit the path → default to the rule of thumb and ask a Champion; do not force firm data into an unapproved tool.
