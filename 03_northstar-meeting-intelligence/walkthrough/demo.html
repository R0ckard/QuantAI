The problem

A memory problem, not a meeting problem

  • Minutes vary by team and can take two hours to prepare.
  • Actions are recorded without owners or due dates, so accountability is weak from the start.
  • Incomplete actions are not carried forward, so they quietly die.
  • Decisions and risks are buried in transcripts and personal notes.
  • Managers cannot see overdue actions across teams.
  • Client-confidential content is copied between tools without a protocol.

The tools are all there. What is missing is a consistent method, a shared accountable record, and a human in control of what gets published.

The approach

A method, an assistant, and registers

Taxonomy & standard
Five meeting types, one minimum record standard: summary, decisions, risks, actions
Intelligence Assistant
Transcript to a structured record in minutes; a human reviews and approves
Registers & dashboard
Actions carried forward; overdue and stuck visible by team

Five-stage workflow: intake, assistant draft, human review, publish, follow-up. Two stages are human by design and never automated: the review, and confirming every action's owner and due date.

AI in action · the record

Owned and dated, never guessed

Transcript excerpt (input)
"...ok so Priya you'll get the revised scope to Meridian by Friday. And someone needs to chase the vendor on that SLA thing. James raised the migration might slip past go-live, we should keep an eye on it..."
STRUCTURED RECORD · reviewer checklist
ACTIONS - Send revised scope to Meridian Owner: Priya · Due: Fri 8 Aug - Chase vendor on SLA breach Owner: [OWNER TO CONFIRM] · Due: [CONFIRM] RISKS - Migration may slip past go-live Severity: [CONFIRM] · Owner: James ⚑ 1 action has no clear owner. Assigned to nobody rather than guessed. ⚑ Nothing published until you approve.

The assistant pulls the actions out with owners and due dates where the transcript gives them, and refuses to guess the one that was left as "someone needs to." It flags it for the reviewer instead. That is what makes "90% of actions owned and dated" real rather than cosmetic. And nothing is published until a human approves.

Governance & what's delivered

Trustworthy, because a human stays in control

The lines we don't cross: nothing is auto-published (a reviewer approves every record), no owner is ever invented (unassigned actions are flagged, not guessed), and client-confidential content follows a clear protocol inside approved tools.
