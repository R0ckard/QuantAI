Turning 120 meetings a week into one accountable memory

A 65-person consultancy was losing decisions and actions in its transcripts: minutes took hours and varied by team, actions had no owner or due date, open work never carried forward, and managers could not see what was overdue. This is the meeting-intelligence system that fixes it, with a human reviewing every record and every action owned, never guessed.

NorthStar Consulting · Prepared by Dave Richardson, QuantAI

NorthStar had a memory problem, not a meeting problem. Across about 120 meetings a week, decisions were hard to retrieve, actions were captured inconsistently and often without an owner or a due date, incomplete actions never made it into the next meeting, and managers had no view of overdue work across teams. The same discussions got repeated because nothing reliable was written down. The tools were all there (Teams, Otter.ai, SharePoint, Planner, Lists, Outlook, Power BI, Claude Team). What was missing was a consistent method and a shared, accountable record, with a human firmly in control of what gets published.

The two prototypes

The Meeting Intelligence Assistant

A Claude Team assistant that turns a transcript into a structured record: summary, decisions, risks, and actions each with an owner and a due date. It never invents an owner (it flags the gap for a human), never publishes on its own, and flags confidential content for careful handling.

The register dashboard

Every action, decision and risk from every meeting in one place. Managers can finally see open, overdue and carried-forward work by team, with repeatedly-slipped items surfaced as stuck. Built as a working, clickable prototype loaded with the 20-meeting pilot data.

The fix that matters most: work that carries forward

NorthStar's worst failure was that incomplete actions never made it into the next meeting, so they quietly died. The system closes that loop explicitly: every action gets a named owner and a due date, open actions are carried forward into the next meeting, and the dashboard counts the carry-forwards so an item slipped twice or more is flagged as stuck. The twenty-meeting pilot alone surfaced six overdue actions and four stuck ones that were previously invisible. Owned · dated · carried forward · visible

The line that makes it safe

In a firm handling client-confidential material, an AI that could publish an unreviewed record would never be trusted. So the design keeps a human in control: nothing is auto-published (a reviewer approves every record), no owner is ever invented (unassigned actions are flagged, not guessed), and client-confidential content follows a clear protocol inside approved tools. The AI removes the two-hour write-up, not the accountability for what was decided.

Why this wins the room. The most valuable output is not a nicer summary; it is an owned, dated, tracked action that carries forward and shows up on a manager's dashboard. That is the difference between "we have minutes" and "we have organisational memory."

Tested on twenty meetings

The workflow was tested across twenty representative meetings spanning the five meeting types: client delivery, internal team, leadership, governance and sales. All five success measures were met, the human-review and confidentiality lines held, and, most tellingly, the pilot made overdue and stuck work visible for the first time. The twenty meetings are the same data loaded in the live dashboard prototype.
