What I'll ask

Six sections. Twenty six questions. About six minutes.

Every answer is a band rather than an exact figure, so you can do the whole thing from memory.

Section 01 · 7 questions

About your firm

Who you are, what the firm does and how many of you there are. Sets the frame for everything after it.

Section 02 · 5 questions

Where the week goes

Which repeat work eats the most time, roughly how many hours it takes across everybody, and who's doing it.

Section 03 · 4 questions

The documents you write twice

File notes, reports, quotes, billing write ups. How long one takes and how many go out a week.

Section 04 · 5 questions

The numbers

What an hour of that time costs, what you bill for an hour, and how much of the week never reaches a client. This is the engine.

Section 05 · 4 questions

Your situation

What made you look at this now, your timing, and who else would be in the decision. Keeps the report pitched where you actually are.

Section 06 · 1 question

One last thing

One open question about what you'd want running by itself. Your answer appears in the report.

Section 4 is the one that matters. The figures in your report are only as good as what goes into them. If you're not sure, pick the band that feels closest. A rough answer in the right band gives you a useful report. A skipped one leaves a hole in it.

Your answers are used to write your report and nothing else. They don't go to anyone else, they're not sold, and you can ask me to delete them by replying to the email that brings the report.

Common questions

What people ask before they start.

Is it actually free? What's the catch?

There's no catch. The check and the report cost nothing. If you want the verified version, that's the workflow assessment at $995, and the report tells you what's in it. You're not obliged to take it and I won't ring you unless you ask.

How accurate is the number?

It's an estimate built from the bands you pick, using conservative midpoints, and it comes back as a range. Its job is to tell you the scale. It isn't a figure I'd put in front of your partners and I say that in the report. The assessment is where it gets verified.

I don't know some of those numbers.

Answer with your best guess. Everything is in bands for exactly that reason, and being one band out won't change what the report tells you.

I already know we've got an admin problem.

Then this is more useful, not less. Knowing something is a problem and knowing what it costs are different things, and the second one is what turns it from an irritation into a priority.

What if the number comes back small?

Then I'll tell you it's small and you've saved yourself a sales conversation. It does happen, and I'd rather write that than talk you into something.

How long does it take to come back?

Two business days. I write every one of them myself, so it isn't an email that fires the second you press submit. If it hasn't landed, reply to the confirmation and I'll chase it.
